# Pichigoose household app

A shared household organizer for families: shopping lists, to-dos, notes and "who owes who", with a scrapbook look and Pichi the goose. Built by Nora, a product designer. She reads code but doesn't write it by hand, so explain things in plain words.

## Read first
- `docs/PRODUCT.md`: what the app does, every user story, the screens and the build phases.
- `docs/DATA.md`: the database plan. Follow it, and ask before changing the shape of a table.
- `docs/DESIGN.md` and `docs/tokens.json`: the look. These are the source of truth.
- `docs/screens/`: the hi-fi screens (images plus the HTML behind them) and wireframes for every other screen.
- `docs/DESKTOP.md`: the desktop layout (sidebar, side panels, the board on home). Desktop screens are in `docs/screens/desktop/`.
- `assets/`: the paper, note, ink and stamp textures, the logo and Pichi.

## Stack
- **Web app first:** React, TypeScript and Vite, installable as a PWA (home screen icon, offline, Web Push). Free to test on iPhone and Android.
- **Backend:** Supabase for Auth, Postgres with Row Level Security, Realtime and Storage. Migrations live in `supabase/migrations`.
- **Hosting:** Vercel.
- **Language:** i18next with `en` (default) and `sr` from day one. No hard-coded interface text.
- **Later:** a Capacitor wrap for the App Store and Google Play. Keep the code free of anything that would block that.

These are proposals except "web app" and "Supabase", which Nora decided. If something here is a bad fit, say why before switching.

## How to work with Nora
1. **No proof, no done.** Never say something is done, fixed or working without showing it in the same message: a screenshot of the screen, or the command output. If you haven't checked, say "I haven't checked this."
2. **Build in phases** (see PRODUCT.md). At the end of each phase, tell Nora how to open it on her phone.
3. **Match the design exactly.** Compare your screen with the hi-fi image side by side before calling a screen finished. The hi-fi HTML in `docs/screens/hifi-source/` has the exact CSS. Reuse it.
4. **Never change Nora's copy.** Use her text word for word. When you write new interface text, keep it short and warm, and list it for her to review.
5. **No em dashes** anywhere: interface, docs or messages. American spelling.
6. **Open questions are hers.** PRODUCT.md lists them. Ask, don't decide.
7. **A question is a question.** If Nora asks "can you" or "is it possible", answer and wait. "Let's plan" or "let's discuss" means no code yet.
8. **Speech to text.** She often dictates, so read for intent and ask only when something truly doesn't parse.
9. **One recommendation, not a menu,** unless she asks for options. Say which suggestions are your own.

## Things that are easy to get wrong
- **Money is personal.** It belongs to the profile, never to a household, and is hidden completely when `track_money` is off.
- **Each member has their own home order** (`household_members.home_layout`).
- **Addresses are three English words** and must be unique. They're generated by a database function.
- **The shopping list must work offline** and sync later. Signal in stores is bad.
- **On iPhone, push only works after "Add to Home Screen".** Guide people there.
- **The headline ink effect must never clip letters.**
- **Avatar lines stay 2 px in rows and headers.** Big avatars (80 px and up) scale their lines and get the headline ink. See DESIGN.md.
- **Pichi is Nora's painting.** Never redraw or generate her.
